\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We compare secret-blind outlines with baseline secrecy instructions, exactly length-matched irrelevant context, and word decoys in a controlled story-writing study. Under the recorded protocol, outlines reduce literal disclosure, including relative to matched context. The primary detection contrast does not establish a reduction in nonliteral hints, and plot-level evidence remains inconclusive. Controlled residual differences preserve readable secret identity, while the fixed raw probe and offline projection checks fail to justify a behavioral intervention.

Follow-up should establish a nonliteral positive anchor with canonical encoding and calibrated judges or blinded human evaluation, then repeat the contrasts with greater power and validated plot alternatives. Causal claims require a context-robust readout and validated online edits with utility noninferiority.
